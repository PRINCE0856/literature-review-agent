# Introduction Template

Structure used by `Introduction.docx`. Every `{placeholder}` is filled from
verified analysis records; a placeholder with no supporting evidence is replaced
by an explicit statement that the evidence base does not cover it — never by
plausible prose.

---

## Scope

This introduction synthesises {n_papers} paper(s) retrieved and analysed for
"{topic}". It addresses: {research_questions}. Only literature published
between {year_from} and {year_to} was considered, and only claims traceable to
a retrieved paper are made.

## Method and evidence base

{n_screened} candidate records were screened, {n_included} were included, and
{n_analysed} yielded readable full text. {n_q1} have a verified Q1 quartile
from a ranking source; {n_unverified} have an unverified quartile and no
quartile has been assumed.

## 1. Background

What the field studies, and the span of the reviewed evidence.
One cited sentence per paper whose research problem could be extracted.

> Rule: no claim about the field's history or size unless a reviewed paper
> states it.

## 2. Why the topic matters

Drawn from the papers' own policy and practice implications.
If no paper states any, say so — and note that this absence is itself recorded
as a policy gap.

## 3. What is currently known

The main findings of the reviewed papers, each cited, with page evidence where
the source supported it.

> Rule: report what each paper found. Do not merge conflicting findings into a
> single smooth statement.

## 4. Methods used in the reviewed evidence

The method families identified, with counts. Cross-reference
`Models_and_Applications.docx` rather than repeating the model detail here.

## 5. Convergence and disagreement in the findings

Where the papers agree, and where they do not.

> Rule: absence of detected contradiction is not consensus. Say the set is
> small and that several papers do not report findings in comparable terms.

## 6. What remains unresolved

Author-stated gaps, quoted and cited. If no paper states a gap, say that the
gaps reported are the agent's inferences from coverage patterns.

## 7. Motivation for further research

What the reviewed evidence leaves uncovered, based on the fields fewer than
two in five papers report, and the contexts listed as under-represented.

## References

Full reference list in {citation_style}, containing only cited papers.

## Limitations

Sample not census; year and language limits; open-access bias; papers that
could not be retrieved; unverified quartiles; PDFs needing OCR; automated
extraction.

## Verification note

Standard note linking every statement to the evidence ledger.

---

## Phrasing rules

| Write this | Not this |
| --- | --- |
| "Within the reviewed evidence, ..." | "Research shows that ..." |
| "In these {n} papers, ..." | "Globally, studies find ..." |
| "{Author} ({year}) reports ..." | "It is well established that ..." |
| "Reading the evidence, ..." (for inference) | Presenting inference as fact |
| "No reviewed paper reports ..." | Filling the gap with a plausible value |
